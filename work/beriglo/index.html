Making Beriglo’s routines easy to buy

Beriglo is an Indian hair and skin care brand built around simple routines. I tried to buy one on their website and it was harder than it should be, so I redesigned how the store works. I kept their fonts, colours and photos (every photo is Beriglo’s own, from beriglo.com) and changed the flow.

What got in the way

Tap the numbers.

    Starting from the problem

    People don’t go looking for a “scalp serum”. They look for help with hair fall. Beriglo’s own routine finder already asks this, and brands like Minimalist and Dot & Key lead with concerns too, so I made it the way in.

    One clear way in

    The homepage now has one main button, Find Your Routine, and a row of concerns right under it. Each one goes straight to a matched routine.

    One downside: the finder lets you pick a second concern, so it needs a “See My Routine” button. That’s one extra tap, but a lot of people have more than one problem.

    Cards you can decide from

    Ratings, product type and key ingredients are on the card now, and the description isn’t cut off halfway through a word.

    Simpler rituals

    There were four buttons that added things to the cart. Now there’s one, “Add Ritual”, and each step says what it does (cleanse, treat, hydrate, protect).

    This matters for the business too: a ritual is Rs. 1,319–1,499, while a single product is Rs. 299–750.

    A cart that helps

    If you add all of a ritual’s products one by one, the cart offers to switch you to the ritual and save Rs. 571. You can undo a removal, and check out without an account.

    In the real store the price mismatch is a Shopify setup issue, so rituals would need to be set up as bundles there too.

    Checking my own build

    I went back to the prototype on a phone-sized screen and did four tasks as a first-time shopper. This was me checking my own work, not a test with real users.

    1. 1

      Find a routine for hair fall and buy it

      Found: it worked in 5 taps, but the Hair and Skin answers looked like plain photos and started below the first screen. The “added to cart” message was squeezed to one word per line, and its “View cart” button was gone after 3 seconds.

      Changed: each answer now says “Choose Hair →” and the photos are shorter, so the first answer fits on screen. The message uses the full width and stays 6 seconds when it has a button. On the routine result, a sticky “Add ritual” bar now sits at the bottom of the screen, so you can buy without scrolling down.

    2. 2

      Remove it, then change my mind

      Worked: removing is instant with an Undo, and the empty cart suggests where to start.

    3. 3

      Check what I’m paying for

      Found: the cart said “3 products” but not which ones.

      Changed: the cart now lists them: Anti-Hairfall Serum + Smoothing Shampoo + Revital Hair Mask.

    4. 4

      Check out and make mistakes

      Worked: no account needed, number keypad for phone and PIN code, and each error says what to fix, like “10-digit mobile number starting with 6, 7, 8 or 9”.

    Still open
    • On a phone, the concern buttons on the homepage start just below the first screen, so you scroll a little before the first tap.
    • Typing a PIN code doesn’t fill in the city and state.

    What I learned

    The Add to Cart button took me four goes.

    1. First
      Jumps to cart page

      ✕ Took people away from browsing.

    2. Second
      ✓ Added

      ✕ Went back to normal after a second, so it looked like nothing happened.

    3. Third
      ✓ 2 in cart

      ✕ Clear, but you couldn’t change the quantity.

    4. Final
      −2+

      ✓ Stays visible and you can adjust it.

    Actually trying to buy something found more problems in ten minutes than looking at the pages did. After the first version worked, I went back through my own build and fixed 24 issues, including low-contrast text and buttons that were too small to tap.

    Next I want to test both versions with five shoppers and time the same task.
