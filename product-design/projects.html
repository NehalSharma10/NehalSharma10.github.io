Magnetic peg board

The magnetic peg board helps patients exercise the wrist and improve finger grip. The patient places magnetic pegs on the board.

There are 25 pegs in 5 different shapes, and each shape matches a different kind of grip.

A task board shows coloured patterns. Each colour stands for a peg, and the patient places pegs to match the pattern. Finishing a task earns the points shown on the task board.

The goal is to motivate patients to practice without a therapist's help, which also saves the therapist time and makes their work easier.

Thoracic X-ray pillow

For patients with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS), doctors assess how flexible the spinal curve is. This helps them understand the curve and plan treatment: which levels to fuse, how much correction to expect, and whether more surgery is needed.

The patient lies on their side on the X-ray table over the fulcrum. The fulcrum sits under the rib at the top of the curve, so the shoulder on that side lifts off the table and the curve bends as much as it can. Hips and knees bend slightly so the patient doesn't roll, and the pelvis and shoulders stay perpendicular to the X-ray beam.

For lumbar curves, the fulcrum goes directly under the top of the curve so the hip bone on that side lifts off the table.

The pillow is designed to be convenient, simple and comfortable for these flexibility assessments.
