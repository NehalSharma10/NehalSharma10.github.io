A healing shoe for diabetic foot

A shoe that helps diabetic patients heal foot wounds and ulcers, using doctor-prescribed medicated patches held exactly where the foot needs them.

TypeMajor project, biomedical product
UsersDiabetic patients with foot wounds or ulcers
OutputWorking shoe prototype
Year2019

Overview

This shoe is designed for diabetic patients to help heal foot wounds and ulcers using medicated patches. The patches carry medicine in the form of a powder or ointment, prescribed by the patient's doctor.

A cavity inside the shoe holds the patches. Its position is decided after examining the patient's plantar pressure, so the medicine sits exactly where the foot needs it. Velcro fixes the patches firmly in place so they don't move while the patient walks.

Problem

Diabetic foot wounds and ulcers heal slowly and need medicine to stay in contact with the wound. Ordinary shoes rub against the wound, shift dressings out of place and are hard to put on and take off over a sore foot.

Research

I measured the plantar pressure of the foot using a plantar pressure scanning machine, and the readings were checked by a doctor. These readings showed which areas of the sole carry the most pressure and decided where the patch cavity is placed in the shoe.

Design features

  • Patch cavity. A space inside the shoe holds the medicated patch, positioned according to the patient's plantar pressure.
  • Velcro-fixed patches. The patches are fixed with Velcro so they stay in place and don't move inside the shoe.
  • Side openings. The shoe opens from both sides, so patients can put it on and take it off easily. Both side openings close with Velcro, which also makes the patches easy to fit.
  • Back opening. An opening at the back lets the patches be fixed properly in position.
  • Prescribed medicine. Each patch carries medicine as a powder or ointment, prescribed by the doctor, to help the wounds and ulcers heal.

The shoe treats the wound while the patient goes about their day, so healing and walking are no longer two separate problems.

Outcome

The result is a working prototype that combines comfort, easy wearing and wound care in one shoe. Designing it taught me to let real measurements, not assumptions, decide the most important parts of a product.
